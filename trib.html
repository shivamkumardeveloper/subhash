Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose

The Great Indian Freedom Fighter

Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom!”


​Early Life & Resignation from ICS: Born on January 23, 1897, in Cuttack, Odisha, Bose cleared the prestigious Indian Civil Service (ICS) exam in England in 1920. However, he resigned in 1921 to join the freedom movement, refusing to serve the British government. ​Congress Leadership & Ideological Rift: He served as the President of the Indian National Congress in 1938 (Haripur) and 1939 (Tripuri). Due to sharp ideological differences with Mahatma Gandhi regarding non-violence and complete independence (Purna Swaraj), he resigned and founded the All India Forward Bloc in 1939. Bose was born into wealth and privilege in a large Bengali family in Orissa during the British Raj. The early recipient of an Anglo-centric education, he was sent after college to England to take the Indian Civil Service examination. He succeeded with distinction in the vital first exam but demurred at taking the routine final exam, citing nationalism to be the higher calling. Returning to India in 1921, Bose joined the nationalist movement led by Mahatma Gandhi and the Indian National Congress. He followed Jawaharlal Nehru to leadership in a group within the Congress which was less keen on constitutional reform and more open to socialism.[x] Bose became Congress president in 1938. After reelection in 1939,
differences arose between him and the Congress leaders, including Gandhi, over the future federation of British India and princely states, but also because discomfort had grown among the Congress leadership over Bose's negotiable attitude to non-violence, and his plans for greater powers
